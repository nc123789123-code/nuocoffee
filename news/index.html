CreditNov 2, 2026

Private credit lenders disagree on what 12% of their shared borrowers are worth

Marks that differ by five points or more now cover 12.3% of borrowers held by several managers, up from 9.3% a year earlier.

What happened

Among borrowers held by two or more private credit managers, the share whose marks differ by five points or more rose to 12.3% as of June 30, from 9.3% a year earlier, according to an analysis by the Frankfurt School of Finance & Management reported by Bloomberg.

Why it matters
  • The same loan carried at different prices means a fund's NAV depends on who is doing the marking.
  • Dispersion tends to widen first in stressed names, before defaults show up.
  • It strengthens the case for independent valuation and fuller disclosure to LPs.
Onlu take

Mark dispersion is an early warning. Before trusting a fund's yield, compare its marks with what co-lenders carry for the same loans.

AIOct 29, 2026

Microsoft borrows $25bn for AI in its first bond sale since 2017

Orders reportedly peaked at $115bn, about 4.6 times the size of the deal.

What happened

Microsoft priced $25 billion of senior unsecured bonds on October 29, its first bond sale since 2017, to help fund AI spending. Orders reportedly peaked at $115 billion, about 4.6 times the deal size. The size and coupon of each tranche had not been widely reported as of October 30.

Why it matters
  • Even the most cash-rich hyperscaler now funds part of its AI capex with debt.
  • Strong demand shows investment-grade buyers still want AI exposure at the top of the capital structure.
  • More hyperscaler issuance competes with every other borrower for investment-grade demand.
Onlu take

The AI build-out has moved from cash flow into the bond market. Hyperscaler spreads are becoming a gauge of AI sentiment.

MacroOct 28, 2026

The Fed holds at 3.75–4.00%, and two officials wanted a hike

A 10–2 vote to hold after September's increase leaves the door open to one more hike in December.

What happened

The FOMC kept the federal funds target range at 3.75%–4.00% on October 28 in a 10–2 vote. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented in favor of a quarter-point increase. The hold followed a quarter-point hike in September; the next meeting is December 9.

Why it matters
  • Dissents in favor of a hike mean the debate is about how much tighter policy gets, not when cuts start.
  • Floating-rate borrowers, a large share of private credit, keep paying today's coupons for longer.
  • Reports after the meeting had markets pricing roughly one more 25 bp hike this year.
Onlu take

Plan for higher for longer. In leveraged credit, interest coverage is the number to watch before defaults.

Barclays seeks insurance on more than $10bn of data center debt

The bank is buying default protection on loans that finance AI infrastructure.

What happened

Barclays is looking to buy default protection on more than $10 billion of debt used to finance AI infrastructure, using credit risk insurance to hedge its data center exposure, Bloomberg reported on October 28.

Why it matters
  • Banks want to keep underwriting AI build-outs without holding all of the risk themselves.

HealthcareOct 19, 2026

FDA grants GSK's Jemperli accelerated approval in earlier-stage rectal cancer

The first approved therapy for stage II and III dMMR/MSI-H locally advanced rectal cancer.

What happened

The FDA granted Jemperli (dostarlimab) accelerated approval for adults with stage II and III dMMR/MSI-H locally advanced rectal cancer, which GSK calls the first approved therapy for that use. It rests on AZUR-1, a single-arm phase II trial of 154 previously untreated patients dosed every three weeks for nine cycles, with clinical complete response at 12 months as the primary endpoint.

Why it matters
  • Patients who reach a complete response may avoid chemotherapy, radiation and surgery.
  • Immunotherapy moving into earlier disease changes the market size and pricing logic.
  • It is an accelerated approval on interim single-arm data; a confirmatory study still has to follow.
Onlu take

Early-stage indications with a clear biomarker are where oncology value moves next. The patient group here is small, so the bigger signal is the path, not the revenue.

ResourcesOct 19, 2026

Brent goes from $84 back to $110 in little more than a week

Tensions in the Strait of Hormuz reversed an early-October slide.

What happened

On October 8, with markets betting on an Iran–Oman deal over the Strait of Hormuz, Brent fell to about $84 a barrel. Tensions then escalated again and Brent passed $110 on October 16, as reported by Fortune, and was still near that level early on October 19. Gold was around $4,190 an ounce and LME copper near $14,500 a tonne.

Why it matters
  • Oil feeds straight into inflation and narrows the Fed's room to ease.
  • A move of about 30% in a week and a half makes hedging and pricing hard for airlines and chemicals.
  • Upstream energy and miners gain cash flow, which helps energy high yield credit.
Onlu take

The geopolitical premium in oil comes and goes too fast to build a long-term view on. Copper is the longer story, driven by grid and data center demand.

ConsumerOct 15, 2026

September retail sales beat, but mostly at the gas pump

Headline sales rose 0.8%; the control group used for GDP rose only 0.1%.

What happened

US retail sales rose 0.8% in September against a 0.3% forecast, with August revised up to 1.3%. Gas station sales jumped 6.4%, adding about 0.55 points. Excluding autos and gasoline, sales rose 0.2%, and the control group used for GDP rose 0.1% against a 0.3% forecast. The figures are not adjusted for inflation, which is still above 3%.

Why it matters
  • Strong nominal sales are not strong real spending; shoppers may be paying more for less.
  • A soft control group suggests consumption added less to third-quarter GDP than the headline implies.
  • Expensive fuel crowds out discretionary spending.
Onlu take

With nominal strength and real weakness, off-price retail should outperform department stores and brands.

CreditOct 14, 2026

Moody's: count PIK and extensions, and private credit defaults go from 4% to 5.8%

Borrowers switching to payment-in-kind look a lot like borrowers that already defaulted.

What happened

Moody's looked at 607 private credit borrowers it holds credit estimates on. In the 12 months to June 2026, 4.0% defaulted; counting PIK amendments and maturity extensions at distressed issuers, the rate is 5.8%. The PIK issuers show weak liquidity, negative free cash flow, high leverage and interest coverage around 1.0x. Business services and software make up half of defaults.

Why it matters
  • The headline rate understates stress: PIK delays a default without fixing cash flow.
  • For funds, PIK income counts as yield but arrives as more debt, not cash.
  • Software and business services are among the sectors private credit lent to most.
Onlu take

Watch PIK as a share of a fund's income before the default rate.

Real estateOct 5, 2026

CMBS delinquency hits its highest since 2020, with office at 12.16%

Multifamily climbed to 8.04% from 6.59% a year ago: the stress is spreading beyond office.

What happened

Per Trepp, overall US CMBS delinquency rose to 8.02% in September (7.85% in August, 7.55% a year earlier), the highest since late 2020. Office rose 16 bp to 12.16%, behind only January's record 12.34%; multifamily rose to 8.04% from 6.59%. The largest single loan was a $1.1bn maturity default on eight Hollywood office and studio properties, since extended 14 months.

Why it matters
  • Extend-and-pretend is running out of road; junior CMBS tranches feel it first.
  • Multifamily is up almost 1.5 points in a year.
  • Retail is the one property type in better shape than a year ago.
Onlu take

Office is a demand problem, not a rate problem. Multifamily deserves more attention because it is a bigger share of loan books.
